## What the escapement is

The Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie (FHH) defines the **escapement** as a mechanism **fitted between the gears and the regulating organ**. Its function, per FHH, is twofold: to **suspend the gears' motion at regular intervals** and to **supply energy to the balance**.

In other words: the mainspring would otherwise release its energy in one uncontrolled rush — the escapement is what paces that flow into even steps, following the beat of the oscillator.

## What it is made of

Per FHH's *Assortiment* entry, the escapement consists of **three parts: the escape-wheel, the lever and the roller**. The [escape wheel](/en/glossary/escape-wheel/) is the part interfacing with the gear train; the lever and roller lead the beat on towards the balance — the oscillator described in [balance & hairspring](/en/mechanisms/balance-and-hairspring/).

## The main types

FHH groups watch escapements into **three main types**:

1. **Recoil escapements** — e.g. the verge or crown wheel;
2. **Dead-beat escapements** — e.g. cylinder, virgule, double virgule;
3. **Detached escapements** — e.g. lever, detent.

Among these, FHH notes the **lever escapement** is "by far the most common today", because it is especially suited to watches and chronometers.

## The beat, per FHH

FHH describes each to-and-fro movement of the oscillator — a "tick-tock" — as **one oscillation**, and each oscillation **comprises two vibrations**. The escapement's lock-and-release follows exactly this beat: the oscillator sets the pace, the escapement feeds the energy that keeps it swinging.
