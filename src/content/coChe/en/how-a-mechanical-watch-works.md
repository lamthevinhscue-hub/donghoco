## The four core components

Every mechanical watch — however cheap or expensive — contains a **going train** of four main components, in sequence:

1. **Mainspring** — the energy source.
2. **Gear train** — transmits the energy.
3. **Escapement** — divides the energy into beats.
4. **Balance wheel** — governs the pace.

Think of each part as a station along the flow of energy.

## The mainspring — stored energy

The mainspring is a long strip of steel coiled around an arbor inside a housing called the **barrel**. When you wind the watch, the spring is coiled tighter — storing energy. As it unwinds, it releases that energy slowly to run the watch.

It works like a **mechanical battery**: you "charge" it by winding, and it "discharges" over many hours — commonly 38–80 hours depending on the watch. That figure is the [power reserve](/en/mechanisms/power-reserve/).

## The gear train — transmitting energy

Energy from the barrel does not go straight to the hands — it passes through **a series of geared wheels** of different sizes. The gear train does two jobs:

- **Transmits** energy from the barrel to the escapement.
- **Slows it down** — large wheels turning slowly drive smaller wheels turning faster, ultimately steering the hour, minute and seconds hands.

## The escapement — dividing energy into beats

This is the cleverest part. Left free, the mainspring would empty in seconds and the watch would spin like a kite. The **escapement** prevents exactly that.

It **divides** the energy into **small, regular beats** — releasing a little at a time, and with each release giving the balance a push to keep it swinging. Read the full mechanism in [the escapement article](/en/mechanisms/escapement/).

## The balance wheel — the timekeeper

The **balance wheel** is a small wheel oscillating back and forth, like the pendulum of a grandfather clock. With every swing, the escapement releases exactly one beat of energy.

The oscillation rate — measured in **vph** (vibrations per hour) — shapes how smoothly the seconds hand moves. Common watches beat at 21,600 or 28,800 vph; some high-end movements beat faster.

## In short

Remember this flow:

**Mainspring** (energy) → **Gear train** (transmission) → **Escapement** (beats) → **Balance wheel** (pace) → **Hands**.

Understanding this flow is the key to every more complex mechanism — from chronographs to tourbillons.
