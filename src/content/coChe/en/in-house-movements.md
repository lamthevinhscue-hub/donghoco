**In-house** describes **how production is organized**: the brand crafts its own movement instead of buying a complete movement from an outside supplier to fit into its watches. The word answers the question **"who made the movement"**, not the question **"how good is the movement"** — two different questions that often get merged into one.

## The FHH definition

The Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie (FHH) records how the Swiss watch industry uses the word "manufacture": it distinguishes brands that **craft almost the entire watch themselves** from workshops that only assemble, regulate and fit the hands, and from brands that assemble watches from bought-in components (the établisseur model). The boundary of the term lies in the **degree of production independence**.

One context note: this definition grew out of the Swiss industry. Applied to German or Japanese brands, the term is read as "degree of production independence" — it serves as a description of how production is organized, not as an official label issued by any body.

## Making your own escapement — a deeper level in the production chain

One step deeper in the chain is making the **escapement** in-house. NOMOS Glashütte is a documented example: the brand calls its in-house escapement the **NOMOS swing system**, and describes it as something almost no other company in the world achieves; the brand further states that **DUW 4601** is its **twelfth in-house calibre**. The source for these brand statements is the product page of the NOMOS Tangente line, listed under Sources below (the model is also profiled on this site, in Vietnamese).

## In-house is not automatically better

This is the point to state plainly: **in-house describes origin, not a quality label**. A self-produced movement can be excellent or unfinished; a movement from a professional specialist supplier can be stable and widely trusted across the industry. Judging a movement means looking at each of its technical decisions — structure, finishing, how accuracy holds over time — not at its origin label.

A related concept: [jewels](/en/glossary/jewel/) — another "countable" movement feature that is often misread as a quality measure.

## Summary

- In-house = the brand produces its own movement; per the FHH definition, the brand crafts almost the entire watch, distinguished from workshops that only assemble and finish.
- Making the escapement in-house is a deeper level of independence in the production chain — its rarity is described by the manufacturer itself: per NOMOS, the swing system is something almost no other company in the world achieves, and DUW 4601 is the brand's twelfth in-house calibre.
- In-house does not automatically mean better: judge a movement by its technical decisions, not by its origin label.
