## What a repeater is

The Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie (FHH) defines a repeater as a watch that **strikes the time on demand**, activated by a **pushpiece or a slide**. It is a **complication** in FHH's sense — a function beyond the hours, minutes and seconds (see [tourbillon](/en/mechanisms/tourbillon/) for another complication in this family).

## The minute repeater — per FHH

Among repeater types, FHH describes the **minute repeater**: the first mechanisms to **precisely indicate the number of minutes elapsed** appeared in the **early years of the 18th century (1700–1710)**, for the most part in **southern Germany**; **Thomas Mudge** has traditionally been credited with the invention of this complication, **circa 1750**.

## Striking automatically vs on demand — per FHH

FHH distinguishes these mechanisms by **how they are triggered**:

- **Automatically** ("passing strike") — the watch strikes as time passes (per FHH);
- **On demand** — striking only when the user activates it.

Within the automatic family, FHH notes:

- **Grande sonnerie** — automatically strikes **both the hours and the quarters** at each quarter, and repeats hours, quarters and minutes on demand (per FHH);
- **Petite sonnerie** — sounds the hours and the quarters **without repeating the hours at every quarter** (per FHH).

FHH also records that the mechanism can be **silenced using an "all or nothing" or "silent" slide**. This article presents both modes within the source's scope and does **not rank** one above the other.
