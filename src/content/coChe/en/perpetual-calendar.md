## What a complication is

The Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie (FHH) defines a **complication** as **any function other than the indication of hours, minutes and seconds**. The perpetual calendar — and the [moon phase](/en/mechanisms/moon-phase/) — both belong to this family.

## The perpetual calendar, per FHH

FHH states that the perpetual calendar is devised to incorporate the **specificities of the Gregorian calendar**. It is called perpetual because it **automatically adjusts to months with 30 days and to the 28 or 29 days in February**. To do this, it incorporates a **mechanical memory whose sequences are repeated every 48 months** to correspond to the cycle of leap years.

In short: it is a calendar complication that accounts for **the different lengths of the months** and for **leap years**, through a mechanical memory running on a 48-month cycle.

## How it differs from an annual calendar

FHH defines the **annual calendar** as a simple calendar that takes automatically into account **months with less than 31 days but not leap years**, and that **must be adjusted once a year**.

The two mechanisms differ in scope — one accounts for leap years, the other does not. This article does **not rank** one above the other; which mechanism a given watch uses is a question for **that model's documentation**.

## The exceptional adjustments — per FHH

FHH notes that a perpetual calendar **must only be adjusted for the exceptionally non-leap years, the next being 2100 and 2400** — exceptions to the leap-year rule of the Gregorian calendar, as stated in the entry itself.
