## What shock protection protects

Inside a mechanical [movement](/en/glossary/movement/), rotating parts turn on very fine staffs held in jewel bearings. When the watch takes a blow, force can concentrate on those staffs.

The Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie (FHH) defines the **shock absorber** as *"a resilient bearing to dampen shocks on the balance-staff pivots"*. In other words: instead of the blow acting directly on the staff, a **resilient** structure sits in between and absorbs it.

## How a shock-protection system works — Incabloc SA's description of its own system

The detail below is **Incabloc SA's description, on its own technical page, of its own Incabloc system** — it is not a description of every shock-protection system:

- The **endstone** and its **bushing** sit inside a **bearing block**.
- The **double-cone director** configuration brings the endstone and bushing **back to centre**, keeping the direction of travel **perpendicular to the balance staff** — in the maker's words, "pivots are therefore not subjected to any stress".
- The **lyre spring** acts "exclusively as a positioning and return force": it has two contact points, and its reserve of elasticity "enables it to react instantly to shocks".
- The system works in **two phases**: the shock energy — transferred by the resistant part of the balance, the staff — is "entirely absorbed by the bearing block (buffer)"; the system then "immediately returns to the exact centre" through the action of the lyre spring.

## The figures Incabloc SA publishes for its own system

- A watch dropped onto the floor from a height of about **one metre** experiences an acceleration of **around 5,000 g**, per the maker's description.
- The **reaction threshold** of the Incabloc shock absorber is **around 35 grams**, per the same description.

Both figures belong to the maker's description of its own system — **they do not carry over to every shock-protection system**, and they are not a durability promise for every wearing situation.

## Incabloc is one specific system, not a generic name

**Incabloc** is the name of one shock-protection system made by Incabloc SA of La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland. FHH's encyclopedia notes: *"The majority of watches today use the Incabloc® system"* — according to FHH. But "the majority" is not "all": which system a given watch uses should be checked in **that model's specification or documentation**, not inferred from a brand name or a price tag.

## The history, as published by the maker

Incabloc SA's history page records:

- **1928** — Fritz Marti begins research into protecting watches against shocks; in April 1928 he builds a watch with a shock-absorbing system "with movable balance jewels".
- **1929** — The first Swiss patent (No. 141098) is registered.
- **1931** — Porte-Echappement Universel SA is founded in La Chaux-de-Fonds (renamed **Portescap** in 1963).
- **1933** — Production of the Incabloc® begins; the name Incabloc® is registered as a trademark on **6 July 1933**.
- **1938** — Final development: the **lyre spring** becomes the symbol of the system.
