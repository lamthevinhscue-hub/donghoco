## What a tourbillon is

Per the Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie (FHH), the tourbillon is a system **devised and patented by Abraham-Louis Breguet in 1801** to **compensate for errors of rate caused by the Earth's gravitational force in upright positions**: the escapement is mounted in a **revolving cage**, with the regulating organ — the [balance](/en/glossary/hairspring/) — at the centre.

The tourbillon is a **complication** in FHH's sense: a function beyond the hours, minutes and seconds.

## How the cage works — per FHH

- The escape-wheel pinion turns about the **fixed fourth wheel**; the cage **generally revolves once a minute** (per FHH).
- Per FHH, this rotation **compensates for errors of rate caused by the vertical position in which pocket watches spend most of their time**. The source frames the mechanism's purpose within that historical context; this article does not extend it to other ways of wearing a watch.
- A tourbillon can contain a **lever or a detent escapement**.

## The karussel — the alternative, per FHH

FHH notes the **karussel** as a **simplified and more robust alternative**: its cage is driven not by the fourth wheel but by the **third wheel**. The two differ in how the cage is driven — this article does not rank them.
