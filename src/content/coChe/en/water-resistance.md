## The core problem

"100m" on a caseback **does not automatically** mean you can swim to 100 metres. The figure on the watch only has meaning **together with the conditions each manufacturer publishes** — and water resistance itself changes with use over time.

## Pressure grows with depth — read the added part right

NOAA (the US ocean and atmosphere agency) describes it: "Pressure increases with ocean depth", and "For every 33 feet (10.06 meters) you go down, the pressure increases by one atmosphere" — roughly every 10 metres of depth, water pressure **adds approximately one atmosphere**. That figure is the added pressure of the water column, not the total pressure at the spot; and it is not a rule for inferring what a watch is allowed to do at any level — conditions of use remain each maker's own publication, as the sections below state.

## Why is there no shared table?

- **OMEGA** — watches are "water resistant **to the depth defined by the model specifications, provided that the components ensuring the water resistance are intact**": the rated depth, conditional on intact sealing components.
- **SEIKO** — a table based on the **BAR marking** on the case, **for Seiko watches**: 3BAR — splashes and rain; 5BAR — plus swimming, yachting and showering; 10/15/20BAR — plus bathing and shallow diving; Diver's 200m — plus scuba diving; Professional 1000m — plus saturation diving.

Two makers, two publications, and no table converts between them — **the manual of the exact model is always the standard**. Seiko's guide itself asks you to read the marking on **your own watch's case** to match its table.

## The standard groups, per FHH

Beyond each maker's own publication, FHH (Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie) describes usage scope by **standard group**:

- **Water-resistant watches** — a watch described as water-resistant, with or without an overpressure indication, must conform to and be tested against NIHS 92-10 (equivalent to the ISO-2281 international standard). This group is meant for **ordinary everyday use, including periods of immersion in water such as leisure swimming**, and can be used under changing air pressure, water pressure or temperature — but it is **not intended for underwater diving**, even with an overpressure indication.
- **Diving watches** — made to be worn underwater at a depth of **at least 100 metres (330 feet)**; they must include a time control device and fully conform to NIHS 92-11 (ISO 6425) in terms of luminosity, shock-resistance, anti-magnetism and the solidity of the strap. The ISO 6425:2018 standard page on iso.org states the same mark: the standard applies to watches "designed to withstand diving in water at depths of at least 100 m", equipped with a secured diving-time system that is visible in darkness; a dedicated annex of the standard covers saturation divers' watches. Read the mark right: this is the standard's scope of application — whether a watch stamped 100 m actually conforms is the maker's publication, not something inferred backwards from the figure on the case.

The unit of measurement is the bar — the FHH entry phrases it as "a unit of pressure where 1 bar equals 1 atmosphere or atm"; a rounded way of speaking within that entry — the two units sit close together but are not strictly identical. Read the levels right: this grouping states scope **at the standard-group level**, while the conditions of use for each specific model remain each maker's own publication above — the two layers do not replace each other.

## The sealing points

Per OMEGA, water resistance is ensured by **gaskets** seated between the **crown, the pushers, the crystal and the case**. Every gap is a way in for water:

- **Crystal** — the gasket around the glass.
- **The [crown](/en/glossary/crown/)** — the most-handled point; a screw-down crown must be screwed tight after every adjustment (OMEGA's rule: "always screw it in after handling it").
- **Caseback** — the gasket around the back cover.
- **Pushers** — OMEGA: "Avoid operating the **chronograph pushers under water**"; the published exception: some Seamaster 300M/600M chronographs can be operated under water.
- **Helium valve** — on some purpose-built dive watches. Rolex describes the saturation-diving context: inside the hyperbaric chamber, the diver's watch gradually fills with helium — its atoms are "so tiny they can penetrate" the case's seals; during decompression, that helium stays trapped in the case, with the risk of a pressure differential against the outside. Rolex's helium escape valve, patented in 1967, is "a safety release valve that activates automatically when the pressure inside the case is too high, allowing the surplus gas to escape". This is how Rolex describes the design for its own models; a helium valve is not a mandatory part of every dive watch.

### Gaskets — the wearing part

Gaskets are rings pressed tight between components. OMEGA states that they "**deteriorate over time**" under temperature, environment and chemicals, and "**must be regularly replaced**" — water resistance is not a fixed property of a watch. Seiko agrees: "A watch's water resistance is **not permanently guaranteed**", affected by **gasket ageing** and **case deformation from shock**.

## Pressure testing

Testing water resistance requires dedicated equipment — leave it to a watchmaker or an authorised service centre. A sourced recommendation (OMEGA, for OMEGA watches): test water resistance **every year**, with a **full service every 5 to 8 years**. Other makers publish their own intervals — read the manual.

## What the makers have published — and what they have not

**Published:**

- Rinse in fresh water after the sea (OMEGA, Seiko); dry with a soft cloth.
- Do not wash with the crown pulled out; avoid washing directly under running water (Seiko).
- Always screw a screw-down crown back after handling (OMEGA).
- Do not operate chronograph pushers under water, except the maker's published exception (OMEGA — some Seamaster 300M/600M).
- 0–60°C as the optimum operating range (OMEGA).

**Not published in the sources above:** the effect of soap/shampoo/pool chlorine on each rating; sauna limits. For these cases, do not infer a rule — read the manual of the model or ask a qualified service.

### Purpose-built dive watches

Each maker's dive line publishes its own dive standards and use table — Seiko's table above, for example, separates Diver's 200m (adds scuba) and Professional 1000m (adds saturation diving) from the regular grades. For a dive watch, read the specification and manual of the exact model — including any standard named on the watch.

## Misconceptions to drop

1. **"50m is fine for swimming"** — it cannot be asserted in general: the allowed-activities table belongs to each maker. Read the manual of the model.
2. **"Water resistant means shower-proof"** — it does not follow from the figure; the conditions of use are the maker's publication.
3. **"It stays water resistant forever"** — no: gaskets age over time; Seiko: "not permanently guaranteed".
4. **"Water resistance covers heat"** — OMEGA lists 0–60°C as the optimum operating range for OMEGA watches; beyond that, there is no general publication.

## Caring for water resistance

- **Test pressure periodically** — annually per OMEGA's recommendation (for OMEGA watches); other intervals per other makers.
- **Close the crown before any water contact** — screw it down (screw-down) or push it flush (plain).
- **Do not operate chronograph pushers under water** — unless the maker states the model allows it.
- **Rinse after the sea** — OMEGA: "Always rinse your watch with tap water after swimming in salt water".

## In short

- The figure on the watch means what its maker publishes — there is no shared table.
- The international standards form two groups per FHH: ISO 2281/NIHS 92-10 for everyday use (including leisure swimming, not for diving) and ISO 6425/NIHS 92-11 for dive watches from 100 m — the grouping does not replace the maker's publication.
- Gaskets at the crown, pushers, crystal and case age over time and need periodic replacement.
- Pressure testing belongs to a watchmaker or the authorised service — annually per OMEGA's recommendation.
