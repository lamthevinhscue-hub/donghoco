A mechanical watch may display time slightly off a chosen reference. This guide is about **recording that trend**: how to observe, what to note down, and what to compare the results against. It is **not** a diagnostic tool for the movement, and **not** a repair guide.

## What the + and − signs mean

When you compare a watch against a reference, the two signs are a **describing convention**:

- **+ (plus):** the watch displays time **ahead of** the reference.
- **− (minus):** the watch displays time **behind** the reference.

They describe fast or slow against the reference you chose — they are **not a conclusion** about the condition of the movement.

## How to observe — following COSC's own suggestions

The COSC FAQ ("How to check a watch's precision?") suggests these neutral ways to check:

- **Choose a reference:** the COSC FAQ mentions comparing against an **atomic clock or a radio-controlled clock** — sources of time that COSC describes as "extremely precise sources of time".
- **Observe over several days:** note the **starting point** (date and time), then note the **end point** and the **difference you observed**. Note down any **conditions of use you want to keep on record** alongside the result. COSC does not prescribe a fixed observing window — pick an interval that suits you and record it clearly.
- **Positions and temperature:** the COSC FAQ suggests trying to see whether different positions or temperature changes affect how the watch runs. This is **observation** — not a way to diagnose a cause, and not a way to adjust anything.

## What to compare the results against

Whatever you record should be **compared against the documentation, warranty terms or certification of that specific watch**:

- If the model carries a certification — for example [Chronometer](/en/glossary/chronometer/) or [Master Chronometer](/en/glossary/master-chronometer/) — compare against the scope that certification publishes.
- Keep in mind: a certification reflects **the result of a test, at a point in time, within that test's scope** — it does not describe the watch's behaviour in every wearing situation. The COSC FAQ puts it this way: the designation is acquired for life, "even if one's performance is likely to change somewhat over time".

## When to talk to a specialist

If your notes show a **long-lasting change**, or if you are simply **unsure**, talk to the **brand's service centre or another qualified specialist**.

What you record at home makes that conversation more concrete (observation period, differences, conditions noted). It does **not** allow you — or anyone — to conclude the condition of the movement from a few observed numbers.
