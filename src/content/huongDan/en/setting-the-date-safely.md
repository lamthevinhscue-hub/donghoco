> Setting the date at the wrong moment can affect the calendar mechanism. The hours to avoid are **not the same across watches** — and there is no way to derive them from a general guide.

## Why is there a "caution zone"?

A calendar watch changes its date **around midnight** — Seiko describes it: "The date changes around **12 o'clock midnight**". To change the date, the calendar mechanism enters its engagement cycle during a span of time around midnight. Quick-setting the date while the mechanism is in that cycle can cause damage — Seiko's guide for **Caliber 6L37** warns verbatim:

> "**CAUTION: Do not set the date between 9:00 p.m. and 1:00 a.m.** Amending the date during this time period may cause problems such as the date failing to change the next day." and "**Avoid amending the date when the watch is displaying between 9 PM and 1 AM. Doing so may cause damage.**"

The essential point: **21:00–01:00 is the window Seiko records in the manual of Caliber 6L37** — one specific calibre. Other calibres — including other Seiko ones — may name a different window in their manuals. **There is no forbidden window that applies to every watch.**

## The single standard: the manual of the exact calibre

Before setting the date:

1. **Identify the exact model and calibre** of your watch (engraved on the case/dial or on the papers).
2. **Find the maker's manual** for that calibre — many makers publish them on their support pages; if the manual names a window to avoid, follow exactly that window.
3. **No manual found?** Ask a **qualified service** (an authorised dealer or watchmaker) — do not derive a safe window from any general guide, including this article.

One point that is sourced from makers' guides: close the [crown](/en/glossary/crown/) after handling — Seiko (6L37): "Return the **screw down crown** fully to its original position"; OMEGA: for a screw-in crown, "**always screw it in after handling it**".

## About calibres with "set-anytime" mechanisms

Some movements disengage the calendar during setting, so the date can be set at any hour. **You can only be sure through the manual of your own model.** Without the manual, ask a qualified service — do not experiment to find out.

## Frequently asked questions

**I already set the date inside the caution zone — is it damaged?**
If the calendar still behaves normally as far as you can observe, no intervention is needed. If you notice anything unusual about the calendar function, stop adjusting and take it to a qualified service.

**What exactly is the caution window?**
It depends on the calibre — there is no shared number. The only sourced example in this cluster: Seiko (Caliber 6L37) names **21:00–01:00** in that calibre's manual; other guides may name different windows. The only standard: the manual of your model; without it, ask a qualified service.

**The date changed to the wrong day on its own — now what?**
Handle it per the manual of the model. Without the manual: ask a qualified service.

**What about quartz watches?**
Follow the manual of the product. Calendar mechanisms in both mechanical and quartz watches have their own change cycle per calibre.

**What about the day-of-week display?**
It usually shares the mechanism with the date — the setting method is per the manual of the model.
