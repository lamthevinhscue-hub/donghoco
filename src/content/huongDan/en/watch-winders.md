> A watch winder is a device that rotates an automatic watch while it is stored. This article neither recommends buying one nor advises against it — it is a frame of questions for determining whether the device suits your watch, and if you use one, what to base the settings on.

## 1. What is a watch winder?

A watch winder keeps an [automatic](/en/mechanisms/automatic-winding/) watch rotating while it sits on a shelf. The mechanism behind it: an automatic watch winds itself through motion acting on a swinging weight, and the energy is stored in the [barrel](/en/glossary/barrel/) — as described in the Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie encyclopaedia (both sources at the end of this article).

This article stops at describing the device and a decision frame: **it does not promise that a winder keeps a watch fully wound or running without stopping** — that depends on the device, the watch model and the settings confirmed for it.

## 2. What to determine before considering one

Four questions, in order:

1. **Is your watch automatic?** This is the basic condition for a winder to be relevant; hand-wound and quartz watches are a different matter (see [winding a mechanical watch](/en/guides/winding-a-mechanical-watch/)).
2. **Does your watch have features that need re-setting if it stops?** Some features take far more effort to re-set than the time of day; that is for you to judge on your own watch, together with [setting the date safely](/en/guides/setting-the-date-safely/) if the date is involved.
3. **What does the manual for your model say?** This is the deciding source: the brand's or movement maker's documentation is the only place that confirms whether your watch may be used with a winder, and how.
4. **Do you actually need the watch kept in a ready-to-wear state?** Answer this by your own needs — there is no universally right answer.

## 3. When it may not be needed

The same frame shows the situations where the device may be unnecessary:

- You do not want another device and power source to manage.
- You are fine winding the watch and re-setting it per the manual each time you wear it again.
- Your answer to 2.4 is that you do not need the watch kept ready-to-wear.

These are all valid personal choices — this article does not rank you into "should" or "should not".

## 4. Before using one

- **Look for a winder section in the documentation for your exact model (or the movement maker).** If the documentation confirms it can be used, follow the settings it gives exactly.
- **If the documentation does not mention it**, do not set operating parameters (turns per day, rotation direction, cycles) based on general advice — ask a professional who knows your watch.
- **Do not infer settings from mechanism theory.** Understanding how an automatic works is not the same as knowing the right configuration for a specific model.

## 5. When the watch is showing unusual behaviour

A winder is not a way to "treat" a watch that is running off, stopping early, sounding odd, or suspected of moisture intrusion. If your watch shows unusual signs, stop considering the device and have the watch examined by a professional instead.

## FAQ

**Does a winder make a watch "healthier"?**
No publicly available source cited in this article asserts that. The device rotates the watch while it is stored; whether to use one for your watch is best answered with the frame in section 2.

**How many turns per day, and in which direction?**
Only per the manual for your model, or the movement maker's documentation. If the documentation does not mention it, do not set it yourself — ask a professional who knows your watch.

**Can a hand-wound watch use a winder?**
A winder is a device for automatic watches. For a hand-wound watch, the winding itself is covered in [winding a mechanical watch](/en/guides/winding-a-mechanical-watch/).
