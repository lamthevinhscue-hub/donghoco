> The water-resistance figure on a dial causes more misunderstanding than any other specification. This guide explains why there is no shared "allowed activities" table — and what actually determines water resistance.

## The most important fact: every manufacturer publishes its own rating and conditions

- **OMEGA** publishes: watches are "water resistant **to the depth defined by the model specifications, provided that the components ensuring the water resistance are intact**" — the rated depth applies while the sealing components remain intact.
- **SEIKO** publishes a table based on the **BAR marking** on the case, **for Seiko watches**: 3BAR — splashes and rain; 5BAR — plus swimming, yachting and showering; 10/15/20BAR — plus bathing and shallow diving; Diver's 200m — plus scuba diving; Professional Diver's 1000m — plus saturation diving.

Two manufacturers, two publications — and no table converts between makers. **The manual of the exact model is the only standard** for what a rating allows, and under which conditions. Seiko's guide itself asks you to read the marking on **your own watch's case** to match its table.

## What actually determines water resistance?

- **Gaskets** — per OMEGA, water resistance is ensured by gaskets seated between the **crown, the pushers, the crystal and the case**. These gaskets "**deteriorate over time**" under temperature, environment and chemicals, and "**must be regularly replaced**".
- **Impacts** — OMEGA: "**heavy impacts** on the crown, pushers or helium valve are known to **affect the water resistance**".
- **Crown condition** — a screw-down [crown](/en/glossary/crown/) must be screwed back after every handling (OMEGA's rule); a plain crown must be pushed flush. Seiko states plainly: "**Do not wash a water resistant watch while the crown is pulled out**". The crown is also the part you handle most — its winding mechanics are covered in [winding a mechanical watch](/en/guides/winding-a-mechanical-watch/).
- **Ageing** — Seiko: "A watch's water resistance is **not permanently guaranteed**."

## What the makers have actually published

- **Rinse after salt water** — OMEGA: "Always **rinse your watch with tap water after swimming in salt water**"; Seiko likewise advises rinsing in fresh water and drying with a soft cloth.
- **Not under running water** — Seiko: "Avoid washing a water resistant watch **directly under running water** — wash it in still water".
- **Chronograph pushers under water** — OMEGA: "Avoid operating the **chronograph pushers under water**". The published exception: some Seamaster 300M/600M chronographs have pushers that can be operated under water — only when the maker states so.
- **Temperature** — OMEGA lists **0–60°C** as the optimum operating range for OMEGA watches. For saunas and very hot water, the sources above publish nothing specific — when in doubt about the conditions, take the watch off.
- **Household chemicals** (soap, shampoo, pool chlorine) — nothing specific is published in the sources above; rinsing with fresh water after exposure is the sourced advice from OMEGA and Seiko for the salt-water case.

## Periodic testing

Per OMEGA: have water resistance **checked every year** at an authorised service centre, with a **full service every 5 to 8 years** — this is OMEGA's recommendation for OMEGA watches; other makers publish their own intervals, per the manual. Pressure testing needs dedicated equipment — leave it to a watchmaker or the authorised service, not a DIY check.

## Signs that water has got in

If you see any of these, **stop wearing it and take it to a watchmaker or a qualified service**:

- A **misty dial**, especially near the crystal.
- Droplets condensed inside the glass.
- The watch **suddenly running off-time**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I shower with a 100m watch?**
There is no universal answer. The rating on the case comes with the conditions its maker publishes — the manual of the exact model is the only standard.

**Why did my watch take in water even though I followed the figure on the case?**
Water resistance depends on gasket condition, crown type and past impacts — and it ages. Each maker publishes its own table (Seiko by BAR for Seiko watches, OMEGA by model specification with conditions) — the manual of the exact model is the standard.

**Does water resistance need periodic testing?**
Yes — a sourced recommendation: OMEGA advises an annual check for OMEGA watches. The interval for your watch: per the maker's manual.

**What about older watches?**
Gaskets age (Seiko: water resistance is "not permanently guaranteed"). For a watch that has not been tested in years, water resistance is **unverified** until it is tested again — even if the case still shows a high figure.
