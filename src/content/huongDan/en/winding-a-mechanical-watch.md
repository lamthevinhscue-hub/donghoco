> Winding is the first skill of owning a mechanical watch — and the first thing every manufacturer's user guide covers.

## Before you start: which kind of watch is yours?

- **A hand-wound watch (manual winding)** — OMEGA defines it as a watch that "**must be wound by hand via the crown** to ensure its continuous operation". You wind it regularly; the specific method belongs to the manual of that model.
- **An automatic watch** — winds itself through the motion of your wrist while worn. Per OMEGA: if the watch **has not been worn for several days**, or your **wrist movement is insufficient**, the power reserve will be **less than its maximum capacity** — "in this case, it is **necessary to wind it manually, following the instructions given in the user manual**".

Before your first winding, read the manual of your model. Every operating detail (direction, turns, stopping point) belongs to each model — no manufacturer publishes a procedure that applies to every watch.

---

## Part 1: Winding a hand-wound watch

### Step 1 — Set up

Wind in a stable place, with dry hands. If the crown turns oddly or feels abnormal, stop and have the watch checked by a qualified service.

### Step 2 — Unscrew the crown (screw-down type)

The [crown](/en/glossary/crown/) usually sits at the 3 o'clock position. On water-resistant cases with a screw-down crown, open the crown the way your model's manual describes — a screw-down crown needs unscrewing before it can be pulled out.

### Step 3 — Wind per the maker's instructions

OMEGA instructs, for OMEGA watches: wind the crown **clockwise** — "When winding the crown clockwise, do you hear or feel a clicking? Mechanical OMEGA watches produce a clicking sound or feel when the crown is wound." The direction and the feel for your model: read the manual.

### Step 4 — How much is enough?

**There is no number of turns or stopping signal that applies to every watch** — the guide of each manufacturer (OMEGA records winding instructions in the user manual of each model) is the only standard. The honest principles:

- **The number of turns and the stopping point: per the manual of the exact model.**
- If the operation ever feels abnormal, **stop and contact an appropriate service** — do not experiment.
- Some designs include their own measures related to a full mainspring, some do not — the behaviour is specific to each calibre.

### Step 5 — Close the crown

Push the crown back to its closed position. For a screw-down crown, OMEGA's rule is explicit: "**always screw it in after handling it**". A crown that does not sit flush with the case is one of the ways water gets in (see [water resistance](/en/guides/water-resistance/)).

## Part 2: Restarting an automatic watch that has stopped

Per OMEGA: an automatic watch that **has not been worn for several days**, or whose wrist movement is insufficient, holds **less than its maximum power reserve** — "in this case, it is **necessary to wind it manually, following the instructions given in the user manual**".

How to do it: wind by hand following the instructions in the manual of your model. Once worn again, the rotor keeps topping up the power reserve through wrist motion.

---

## Part 3: When does a watch need winding?

| Situation | Principle |
|------------|-----------|
| **Worn every day (automatic)** | Wrist motion maintains the power reserve; when it is sufficient, hand winding is not required |
| **Not worn for days / stopped** | Wind by hand to restart — per the manual (OMEGA records this explicitly) |
| **Hand-wound watch** | Wind regularly at the rhythm given in the manufacturer's manual |

There is no universal number of wear-hours or "safe days" — the actual power reserve depends on the calibre and on how you wear the watch.

## What to avoid

- ❌ **Forcing the winding** — the number of turns and the stopping point belong to the model's manual; forcing is not in any maker's instructions. Anything feels abnormal → stop and contact an appropriate service.
- ❌ **Handling near water with the crown open** — for a screw-down crown, always screw it back after adjusting (OMEGA's rule); for a plain crown, push it flush against the case.
- ❌ **Assuming every calibre works the same** — crown positions and the winding method differ by calibre. When in doubt, read the manual.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you overwind a watch?**
Never force it. Some designs include their own measures when the mainspring is fully wound, some do not — the number of turns and the stopping point are per the model's manual; anything feels abnormal → stop and contact an appropriate service.

**Do you pull the crown out to wind?**
The crown position used for winding and the number of positions are a design of each calibre — read the manual of your model.

**Does an automatic watch need hand winding?**
Yes — per OMEGA, after several days unworn, or with insufficient wrist movement, the power reserve drops below its maximum and the watch **needs to be wound manually per the user manual**.
