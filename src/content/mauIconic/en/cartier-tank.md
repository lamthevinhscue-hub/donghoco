## Origins

In 1917, in the middle of World War I, Louis Cartier — struck by the newly arrived Renault FT-17 tank — sketched a watch whose form drew directly from the machine: a rectangular case standing in for the tank's body, and the **brancards** (side bars) on either side for its tracks.

It was an aesthetic revolution. Wristwatches of the era were mostly round, echoing the pocket watch. The Tank proved a wristwatch could be **a work of design in its own right**.

## Design signatures

- A **rectangular case** with perfectly balanced lines.
- The **brancards** — two metal bars flanking the case, extending through the strap.
- The **chemise de fer** (railroad track) minute ring circling the dial.
- **Sword-shaped hands** — épée.
- A **sapphire cabochon** crowning the crown — a Cartier signature.

## Enduring appeal

The Tank has been worn by **Andy Warhol, Jacqueline Kennedy, Princess Diana, Clark Gable, Catherine Deneuve, Muhammad Ali, Rudolph Valentino** — and more recently **Ralph Fiennes and Tom Hanks**. No other dress watch carries such a roster.

> "I don't wear the Tank to tell the time — I wear it because it is a piece of art." — Andy Warhol (as widely quoted)

## Main variations

- **Tank Normale** (1917) — the original, never mass-produced in great numbers.
- **Tank Cintrée** (1921) — curved to hug the wrist; rare and eagerly collected.
- **Tank Louis** (1922) — softer corners, more formal.
- **Tank Française** (1996) — the sportier version with an integrated bracelet.
- **Tank Must** (1977) — the accessible line that helped carry Cartier through the Quartz Crisis.

## The movement

The Tank has carried many generations of calibres — from classic hand-wound movements, through quartz during the crisis years, back to slim in-house mechanics such as the **8971 MC** and the manual-wound 0501 for high-end versions.

## Its place in history

If the Submariner defined the sports watch, the **Tank defined the dress watch** — proof that a watch can be more than an instrument: it is a statement of the wearer's taste.
