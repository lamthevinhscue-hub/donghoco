## Origins

In 1948, right after World War II, the British Royal Air Force needed a new pilot's watch — accurate, durable and antimagnetic (the cockpit was full of electromagnetic equipment). IWC won the contract alongside JLC and a few other makers, and the **Mark XI** was born.

The Mark XI became the standard watch for RAF pilots and Commonwealth air forces for more than 30 years. It is the model that defined the **"pilot watch"** — the benchmark for every pilot watch that followed.

## Design signatures

The Mark XI shaped the "Flieger" (German pilot) aesthetic and the pilot watch in general:
- **A black dial** with large white Arabic numerals under lume — instantly legible.
- **Thick white lume-filled plier-shaped hands**.
- **The double triangle at 12 o'clock** — the classic pilot-watch signature.
- **A 36mm steel case** — small by today's standards, but ideal for a pilot.
- **An oversized crown** — easy to operate while wearing flight gloves.
- **A soft-iron case** — shielding the movement from cockpit magnetic fields.

## Anti-magnetic — the key point

Cockpits of that era were full of electromagnetic equipment that could disrupt a mechanical watch. The Mark XI has a **soft-iron cage** around the movement, protecting it from fields of around 1,000 gauss. It is the feature that sets the Mark XI apart from an ordinary dress watch.

## The evolution of the Mark line

- **Mark XI** (1948–1981) — the original military issue, extremely rare.
- **Mark XII** (1994) — the first civilian version, using the JLC calibre 889.
- **Mark XV** (1999) — a larger version (38mm), using the ETA 37524.
- **Mark XVI** (2006) — a bigger case (40mm).
- **Mark XVIII** (2016–present) — the current generation, 40mm.

## The movement

The original Mark XI used IWC's **calibre 89** — hand-wound, durable, accurate. The later civilian generations used the JLC calibre 889, then a modified ETA 2892.

## Its place in history

The Mark XI is **the prototype of the pilot watch** — it defined the aesthetics and the functions that every later pilot watch, from the IWC Big Pilot to Laco and Stowa, inherited. Owning a vintage Mark XI is one of the great prides of pilot-watch collecting.
