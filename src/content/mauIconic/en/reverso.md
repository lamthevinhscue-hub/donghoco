## Origins

In 1930, in British India, English officers playing polo kept running into the same problem: the watch glass shattered when the ball was struck during a match. One of them asked René-Alfred Chauvot — a French engineer — to find a solution.

In 1931, Chauvot patented an ingenious mechanism: a **watch face that pivots 180°** on its axis, tucking the dial inside the case and leaving the steel back to take the blows. That was the birth of the **Reverso** (Latin: "I turn around").

## Design signatures

- **An Art Deco rectangular case** with the three distinctive vertical grooves on the back.
- **A slide-and-flip mechanism** — the case slides up its rail and rotates 180° on the axis, no screws involved.
- **A restrained dial** — baton or Arabic indices, nothing fussy.
- **An engravable back** — the tradition of engraving names, crests or miniature paintings on the reverse.

## Cultural meaning

The Reverso is more than a watch — it is **a certificate of merit in metal**. Many owners engrave wedding dates, children's names, family crests, or even miniature portraits in the art of **enamel** (a Jaeger-LeCoultre high-complication tradition). A Reverso often becomes a family heirloom.

## The main lines

- **Reverso Classique** — faithful to the 1931 original.
- **Reverso Tribute** — larger, with variants such as the Chronograph and the Duodate (two time zones).
- **Reverso Grande Complication à Triptyque** — three faces with 18 functions, a horological masterpiece.
- **Reverso One** — the ladies' line, in smaller sizes.

## The movements

Reverso cases are thin, so the calibres are **hand-wound**; notable ones include the **854A/2** (thin, hand-finished) and the **754** (automatic, with elongated bars transmitting power across the dial face). Notably, the double-faced Reverso Duoface runs on a single calibre driving two separate dials.

## Its place in history

Alongside the Cartier Tank, the Reverso is **an icon of the Art Deco era's rectangular dress watches**. What makes it unique: it pairs a **practical function** (protecting the dial) with **personalization** (the engravable back) — a combination few watches can claim.
