## Origins

In 1953, as scuba diving was taking off, the world needed watches that could survive deep-water pressure. Rolex answered with the **Submariner** — according to Rolex, the first dive watch water-resistant to 100 metres. The earliest reference 6204 examples are dated to late 1953; Rolex presented the model at Basel in 1954.

The Submariner was not the first dive watch, but it set the **template for the whole category**: a unidirectional rotating bezel, water resistance of 100 metres or more, and a dial legible in the dark.

## Design signatures

- **Unidirectional rotating bezel** with a 60-minute scale — it turns one way only, so an accidental knock can only shorten apparent dive time, never extend it (the safe direction).
- **Mercedes hands** on the hour hand — a Rolex signature.
- **Chromalight** blue luminous dots for darkness legibility.
- The three-link **Oyster bracelet**.

## Key references

- **6204** (1953) — the first reference to carry "Submariner" on the dial; earliest examples dated to late 1953.
- **5513** (1962–1989) — no-date, non-chronometer, produced for 27 straight years.
- **14060 / 14060M** — the "No Date" generations, prized for their symmetry.
- **114060** (2012–2020) — the super-case generation.
- **126610LV "Starbucks"** (2020–) — the current generation, with green bezel.

## The movement

Modern Submariners use Rolex in-house calibres: **3230** in the no-date and **3235** in the Submariner Date — both self-winding, with a power reserve of around 70 hours, certified to the Superlative Chronometer standard of −2/+2 seconds per day after casing. Both use the patented **Chronergy escapement** and the blue antimagnetic **Parachrom** hairspring.

## Its place in history

The Submariner is more than a dive watch. It appeared on James Bond's wrist in the early-1960s films, became shorthand for adventure, and is today **the most widely recognized luxury sports watch in the world**.
