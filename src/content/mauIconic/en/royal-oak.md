## Origins

In 1972, the Swiss watch industry was in the grip of the Quartz crisis. Audemars Piguet needed a lifeline product. They turned to **Gérald Genta** — and in a single evening he sketched the Royal Oak: a **stainless steel** sports watch priced **on par with gold watches**.

It was a wild gamble. At launch the Royal Oak was mocked — "who pays gold money for a steel watch?" Yet within a few years it had come to define an entire segment.

## Design signatures

- **Octagonal bezel** with eight exposed screws — a shape reminiscent of diving helmets and ship anchors.
- **Screw-through construction** — the screws pass through the bezel into the case, a one-of-a-kind feature.
- **"Grande Tapisserie" dial** — a large waffle pattern, made in several sizes, that comes alive as the light changes.
- **Integrated bracelet** — a single flowing piece that thins toward the clasp.
- **Baguette hands and indices** — long and slender, with a subtle layer of luminous coating.

## The name

"Royal Oak" takes its name from **the ships of the British Royal Navy** — HMS Royal Oak among them, whose portholes were octagonal like the bezel. As for reference "5402", Gérald Genta chose the number because he considered it lucky.

## Reference generations

- **5402** (1972–1979) — the original 39mm "Jumbo", Genta's design, extremely rare.
- **14802** (1992–2000) — the 20th anniversary edition, with a sapphire caseback.
- **15202** (2012–2021) — the modern take on the 39mm Jumbo, ultra-thin automatic.
- **16202** (2022–present) — the current generation, with the new calibre 7121.

## The movement

The Royal Oak "Jumbo" runs the ultra-thin automatic calibre **7121** (the new generation, 2022), just 3.2mm thick with a 52-hour power reserve — one of the thinnest automatic calibres in the world.

## Its place in history

The Royal Oak **changed the rules of the industry** — proving that steel could be more luxurious than gold. Together with the Nautilus (1976) and later the Vacheron Constantin Overseas, it opened the **"luxury sports"** segment — the most in-demand segment of high-end watchmaking today.
