## Origins

1969 was the **decisive year** for the modern chronograph. Three camps raced to be first with an automatic chronograph: Heuer–Breitling–Hamilton (Caliber 11), Seiko, and Zenith.

Zenith launched the **El Primero** (Spanish: "the first") — and it was a genuine technical leap. Not only an automatic chronograph, the El Primero was also **integrated** (designed as a chronograph from the ground up, not a bolt-on module) and ran at a high frequency of **36,000 vph** (5 Hz) — one and a half times the usual rate.

## The technical breakthroughs

- **An integrated automatic chronograph** — one of the first movements of this kind.
- **A 36,000 vph frequency (5 Hz)** — above the usual 28,800 vph, giving a smoother, finer-grained seconds hand.
- **A 50-hour power reserve** — respectable for its day.
- **A date** — built into the calendar.

## The miraculous survival story

During the Quartz crisis of the 1970s, the company decided to **destroy** the El Primero tooling and drawings — mechanical movements were declared obsolete. But one watchmaker, **Charles Vermot**, refused. He **secretly hid** the entire set of machines, drawings and stamping dies in the factory attic, defying the board's orders.

When mechanical watches revived in the 1980s, the El Primero returned to production **thanks to Vermot's act**. Without him, the movement might have vanished for good. His story has become one of the industry's most beloved legends.

## Rolex put the El Primero in the Daytona

In 1988, Rolex needed an automatic chronograph movement for the Daytona (until then it had used hand-wound Valjoux calibres). Rolex chose the **El Primero** — modified (the frequency reduced from 36,000 to 28,800 vph) and renamed "calibre 4030". Rolex used it in the Daytona for **twelve years** (1988–2000), until the in-house calibre 4130 arrived.

It is the strongest evidence of the El Primero's quality: **even Rolex used it**.

## Reference generations

- **A386** (1969) — the original El Primero, with the "three-colour" dial, extremely rare.
- **A3817** (1971) — a larger version.
- **El Primero 3600** (2019) — the new-generation calibre, displaying 1/10th of a second.
- **Chronomaster Sport** (2021) — the current generation, 1/10th of a second.

## Its place in history

The El Primero is one of the **longest-running chronograph movements still in production**. It proves that good engineering can outlive its era — and it remains a point of pride for the entire Swiss watch industry.
