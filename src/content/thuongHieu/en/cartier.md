## Overview

Cartier came to watchmaking **from the opposite direction to every Swiss brand**: it is a Paris jewellery house, founded in 1847, that built its reputation on jewellery before moving into watches in the early 20th century.

That is why Cartier thinks in **shape first, mechanism second**. The rectangular Tank, the rounded-square Santos, the distorted Crash, the oval Baignoire — each is a silhouette you recognise from across the room, without seeing the logo. Cartier's biggest historical contribution was **moving the watch from the pocket to men's wrists** at a time when the wristwatch was still considered a women's jewellery item. In 1988, Richemont took full control of Cartier.

## Watchmaking philosophy

- **Design leads.** At Cartier the case shape comes first and the movement must fit it, not the other way around — which is why the brand has so many unusually shaped, non-round movements.
- **A near-century of consistent visual language.** Roman numerals, a chemin-de-fer minute track, blue lacquered sword hands, a cabochon-set crown — this code has barely changed since Louis Cartier.
- **Geometry as the through-line.** Cartier does not chase the round sports-watch trend; it has persisted with shaped cases for over a hundred years.

## Key lines

- **Tank** (1917) — the number-one icon, a rectangular case inspired by the Renault FT-17 tank.
- **Santos** (1904) — the line with the oldest historical roots, made for the pilot Alberto Santos-Dumont; a rounded-square case with exposed screws.
- **Santos-Dumont** — a thinner, more elegant version, usually hand-wound.
- **Ballon Bleu** — the only round line in the icon group.
- **Panthère** — a jewellery line with a soft linked bracelet.
- **Crash** — a rare collector's piece with a distorted, asymmetrical case (introduced by Cartier London, 1967).
- **Baignoire** — an elongated oval case.
