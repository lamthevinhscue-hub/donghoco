## Overview

Omega was founded in 1848 at La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland, by Louis Brandt. Over a long history, the brand has attached its name to landmark events: official timekeeper of the Olympic Games, and maker of the only watch to have reached the surface of the Moon.

## Key milestones

- **1848** — Louis Brandt opens his assembly workshop at age 23, initially sourcing parts from local craftsmen.
- **1894** — The outstanding "Omega" pocket calibre gives the brand its name: the last letter of the Greek alphabet, meaning the summit.
- **1905** — First Olympic Games as official timekeeper — the start of a sporting link now spanning 30 Games.
- **1957** — The **Speedmaster** launches alongside the Seamaster 300 and Railmaster as the brand's "professional" trio.
- **1969** — **Apollo 11**: Buzz Aldrin wears his Speedmaster Professional on the lunar surface — the first and only watch worn on the Moon. NASA's brutal testing eliminated rival brands, leaving one survivor.
- **1995** — James Bond switches to Omega (GoldenEye): the start of a three-decade Seamaster association.
- **1999** — The **Co-Axial escapement**, conceived by George Daniels, goes into series production — the most significant escapement departure in 250 years, with reduced friction and less dependence on lubrication.
- **2015** — **Master Chronometer**: the METAS-certified standard of 0 to +5 seconds per day **and** resistance to 15,000 gauss.

## Watchmaking philosophy

Omega pairs its long history with continuous technical renewal: pioneering accuracy certification (chronometry), antimagnetic performance (Master Chronometer) and new materials (ceramic, silicon, proprietary alloys).

## Key lines

- **Speedmaster** — the "Moonwatch" chronograph (read the profile: [Omega Speedmaster](/en/iconic-watches/omega-speedmaster/)).
- **Seamaster** — divers and all-rounders, Bond's choice since 1995.
- **Constellation** — the elegant line with its star emblem and griffes.
- **De Ville** — refined dress watches.
