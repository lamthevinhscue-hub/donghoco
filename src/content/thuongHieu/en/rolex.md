## Overview

Rolex is the most recognized watch brand on the planet — yet what outsiders rarely know is that the company **belongs to no one but a charitable foundation**: the Hans Wilsdorf Foundation, created in 1945. Rolex is not listed on any exchange and publishes no financials. Its century-long strategy has not changed: build durable, accurate, hard-wearing tool watches, improving incrementally rather than revolutionarily. Rolex also runs one of the industry's most vertically integrated production chains — it even casts its own gold and its own Oystersteel (a 904L steel) in dedicated foundries.

## Key milestones

- **1905** — Hans Wilsdorf & Alfred Davis found the company in London; Wilsdorf believes in the wristwatch while the industry still sees it as jewelry.
- **1908** — The "Rolex" name is registered: short, pronounceable in every language.
- **1914** — A Rolex wristwatch earns Class A precision certification from the Kew Observatory — a standard previously reserved for marine chronometers.
- **1926** — The **Oyster**, the first mass-produced waterproof case, sealed by a screwed-down caseback and crown.
- **1927** — Mercedes Gleitze swims the English Channel wearing an Oyster; Rolex buys the front page to tell the story — the birth of the "Testimonee" model of marketing.
- **1931** — The **Perpetual** rotor: a 360° self-winding system that remains the industry's template.
- **1953** — The **Explorer** (linked to the Everest expedition) and the **Submariner** (waterproof to 100m) launch.
- **1955** — The **GMT-Master** is created for Pan Am pilots.
- **2015** — The modern **Superlative Chronometer** standard of ±2 seconds per day, alongside calibre 3235 with the Chronergy escapement.

## Watchmaking philosophy

- **"Perpetual"** — lasting reliability above complication for its own sake.
- An internal **Superlative Chronometer** standard of −2/+2 seconds per day after casing — twice as strict as COSC.
- **Marketing through real expeditions** — the Channel (1927), Everest (1953), the Mariana Trench (1960) — rather than pure advertising.
