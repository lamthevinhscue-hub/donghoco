## Overview

TAG Heuer is the name most tightly bound to **sports timing and motor racing** in Swiss watchmaking history. The "TAG" appeared in 1985 when the Techniques d'Avant Garde group acquired Heuer; from 1999 the brand has belonged to LVMH. Its two icons — the Carrera (1963) and the Monaco (1969) — were both born on the racetrack.

## Watchmaking philosophy

- **"Avant-garde"** — pioneering in timekeeping: from the oscillating pinion patent (1887), still used in chronographs today, to the Mikrograph stopwatch accurate to 1/100th of a second (1916).
- **Bound to speed sports** — official timekeeper of the Olympic Games of the 1920s (Antwerp 1920, Paris 1924, Amsterdam 1928); today tied to Formula 1.
- **A "gateway" into fine Swiss watchmaking** — solid chronographs at the most accessible prices among the major brands, from the entry-level Formula 1 line to the high-end Carrera Heuer 02.

## Key milestones

- **1860** — Edouard Heuer opens his workshop in St-Imier, aged 20.
- **1887** — Patent for the **oscillating pinion**, a key chronograph component the industry still uses more than 130 years later.
- **1916** — The **Mikrograph**, the first stopwatch accurate to 1/100th of a second.
- **1962** — The **Autavia**, the brand's first wrist chronograph with a rotating bezel (AUTomobile + AVIAtion).
- **1963** — Jack Heuer creates the **Carrera**, inspired by the Carrera Panamericana road race.
- **1969** — The **Monaco** with the Calibre 11 — co-developed with Breitling, Hamilton-Buren and Dubois-Dépraz, one of the world's first automatic chronographs, in the first water-resistant square case.
- **2010** — The in-house chronograph **Calibre 1887**.
- **2015** — The **TAG Heuer Connected** — the first major Swiss brand to commit to a smartwatch.
- **2017** — The **Heuer 02** calibre (80-hour reserve) and the return of the Autavia.
