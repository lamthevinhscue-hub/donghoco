## Overview

Vacheron Constantin was founded in **1755** in Geneva by Jean-Marc Vacheron — making it **the world's oldest watchmaker in continuous operation**. Aged 24, Vacheron signed the contract for his first apprentice, the earliest document recording the workshop. The brand belongs to the "Holy Trinity" alongside Patek Philippe and Audemars Piguet. Its emblem, registered in 1880, is the **Maltese cross** — inspired by the emblem of the Geneva district where the manufacture worked in the 19th century.

## Watchmaking philosophy

Vacheron pursues the motto *"Faire mieux si possible, et toujours bien faire"* — "Do better if possible, and that is always possible", coined in 1819 when François Constantin joined the house. It is one of the few brands to earn the **Geneva Seal** (Poinçon de Genève) on most of its products — a demanding finishing certification. The manufacture builds its own movements, including some of the most complicated: the **calibre 2755** (tourbillon, minute repeater, perpetual calendar) and, in 2015, the **Reference 57260** — with 57 functions, the most complicated watch ever made, built for three private clients.

## Key lines

- **Overseas** — steel sport-luxury with a six-sided "Maltese cross" case, competing directly with the Nautilus and Royal Oak.
- **Patrimony** — pared-down elegant dress watch, redefined in 2003.
- **Traditionnelle** — the classic line with grand complications.
- **Historiques** — reissues of legendary classics (the American 1921 and others).
- **Métiers d'Art** — peak decorative crafts (enamel, engraving).

## Key milestones

- **1819** — François Constantin joins; the motto is coined.
- **1839** — Georges-Auguste Leschot invents a pantograph for precise movement production, putting the firm ahead of industrialisation.
- **1955** — Calibre 1003, a 1.64mm ultra-thin hand-wound movement — the thinnest in the world at the time — marks the bicentenary and lays the ground for the Patrimony line.
- **1996** — The Overseas launches into the steel sport-luxury wave.
- **2015** — Reference 57260, the most complicated watch ever made.
