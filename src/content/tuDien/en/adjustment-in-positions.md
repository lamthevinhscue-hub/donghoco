A watch is described as "adjusted to positions" — where does that number come from, and who sets it? There is no single answer: only the separate publications of individual makers, and this entry presents exactly two of them.

## Grand Seiko — a six-position standard

Grand Seiko's inspection standard page publishes: "Mean daily rate in six positions"; "Number of positions in inspection — 6 positions"; and lists them: "(Dial Up, Dial Down, 12 o'clock Up, 3 o'clock Up, 6 o'clock Up, and 9 o'clock Up)". The source also explains: "in the GS inspection, 12 o'clock Up position… is added, six orientations are specified" — the 12 o'clock Up position is what Grand Seiko adds to the common split, giving **six orientations** in total.

## Patek Philippe — a precision-adjusted phase

Patek Philippe's official instruction manual (Caliber 240) publishes: "The rate accuracy of Patek Philippe watches is verified in several phases of manufacturing, both with uncased and fully cased movements", and "…its rate accuracy will be monitored and **precision-adjusted** for a further period of two weeks". This publication describes the fine-adjustment phase; it **states no position count**.

## Two publications, two scopes

Grand Seiko shows its standard's list of positions; Patek Philippe shows its process's adjustment phase. Each publication stands in its own scope — read them side by side to compare wording, not to merge into one standard.
