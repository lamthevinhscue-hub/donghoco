## The scope of this entry

The P0-D1 source record notes that no official source defines "adjustment in positions" as a common standard. This entry therefore **does not define the concept** — it presents two specific publications, each with its own scope: Grand Seiko publishes measurement/inspection in **six listed positions**; Patek Philippe publishes a **precision-adjusted** phase of two further weeks, with no position count stated. The entry title is a term's name, not a technically sourced definition.

## Grand Seiko — a published six-position standard

Grand Seiko's inspection-standard page states: "Mean daily rate in six positions"; "Number of positions in inspection — 6 positions"; and lists them: "(Dial Up, Dial Down, 12 o'clock Up, 3 o'clock Up, 6 o'clock Up, and 9 o'clock Up)". The source explains: "in the GS inspection, 12 o'clock Up position… is added, six orientations are specified" — the 12 o'clock up position is Grand Seiko's own addition to the common set, giving **six orientations**.

## Patek Philippe — a published precision-adjusted phase

Patek Philippe's official instruction manual (Caliber 240) states: "The rate accuracy of Patek Philippe watches is verified in several phases of manufacturing, both with uncased and fully cased movements", and "…its rate accuracy will be monitored and **precision-adjusted** for a further period of two weeks" — the rate is monitored and fine-tuned for two further weeks. This publication describes the adjustment phase; it **does not state a number of positions**.

## Reading limits

- The two sources are **two separate maker publications** — they are not merged into a common standard, and no "industry" number of positions is derived.
- "Adjusted in positions" in the title is the concept's name; the content asserts only what each source states.

## In short

- Grand Seiko: the brand's inspection standard specifies **six named positions**.
- Patek Philippe: rate accuracy is verified in several manufacturing phases and **precision-adjusted** for two further weeks (no position count stated).
- Two publications, two scopes — not one common standard.
