A timing machine prints out a rate of −4 seconds per day for your watch — but what about the amplitude figure next to it? To read it, start with what the word actually means. FHH (Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie) defines amplitude: "The distance between the two extreme points of a movement or periodic phenomenon (a angle)" — the **distance between the two extreme points** of an oscillation, expressed as an **angle**.

## How it is measured

Amplitude is one of the standard readings a timing machine (timegrapher) displays. Witschi, a maker of timing instruments, lists "Amplitude" as a measured value on its ChronoMaster line, with resolution given in degrees. In other words: it is a quantity read with **dedicated equipment**, not by eye. On the maker's page, it appears in the same list of measured values as the rate — each value is one line in the device's readout.
