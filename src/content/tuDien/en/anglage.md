**Anglage** (beveling, chamfering) is **beveling the edges** of [bridges](/en/glossary/bridge/) and other movement parts, then **polishing them mirror-bright**. It sounds simple — yet it is the **clearest dividing line** between genuine hand finishing and industrial finishing.

## Two surfaces: outer and inner

Every bridge edge has an **outer face** (a machine can bevel it) and an **inner angle** — the sharp, recessed corner where two surfaces meet, **out of a machine's reach**. This is the spot that must be done **entirely by hand**: the finisher works each corner with hand tools and polishes until the reflection comes back sharp.

That is why anglage serves as the standard test in finishing certifications (such as the Poinçon de Genève): look at whether the inner angles are rounded and polished, and the workshop's level of skill shows.

## Degree and meaning

On industrially finished watches, edges are left rough or lightly chamfered. On high-end watches, edges get a **wide bevel** (45 degrees, even in width) and a **mirror polish** — a bright line running the length of the bridge. Keeping that width even, with no wide-then-narrow wandering, demands real skill.

Anglage does not make a watch keep better time — it is a **statement about the maker's attitude**. You are paying for work in places most people will never see.
