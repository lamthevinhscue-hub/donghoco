How many times a year do you correct the date for the 30-day months — and then once more at the end of February? The **annual calendar** exists to cut that chore down. Patek Philippe, the maker that invented the complication, publishes on its official page: "The most famous of our "useful complications" is the annual calendar, invented and patented by Patek Philippe", and "Introduced in 1996, this mechanism provides an ingenious answer to the problem of date display. It indicates the date for months of 30 and 31 days and needs only one correction per year, at the end of February".

In short: the annual calendar shows the correct date for 30- and 31-day months and **needs one correction per year** — at the end of February. FHH (Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie) has an annual calendar entry to the same effect: the calendar accounts for the shorter months and needs adjusting once a year.

## Where it differs from the perpetual calendar

The difference sits in **February**: the annual calendar needs one correction a year, while the perpetual calendar also handles February's 28/29 days on a four-year cycle. The perpetual calendar has its own entry: [Perpetual Calendar](/en/glossary/perpetual-calendar/) — this entry does not repeat it.
