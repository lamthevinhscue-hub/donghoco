The **barrel** is the **round housing** that contains the [mainspring](/en/glossary/mainspring/). In the Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie's description, the spring is hooked to the barrel at its outer extremity and to the **barrel arbor** at its inner extremity; the toothed wheel on the outside of the barrel **meshes with the first pinion of the gear train**.

## How it works

As the mainspring unwinds, the barrel turns very slowly — FHH puts its rotation at **from one-ninth to one-sixth of a revolution per hour**. That slow, continuous motion is what transmits energy to the whole watch.

The barrel arbor supports the barrel and its spring: a cylinder with a hook for the spring's inner end, its upper pivot cut into a square for the winding wheel.

## One barrel or several?

The number of barrels — one, two, or more — and how they are combined are **design decisions of each calibre**. They belong to the maker's choices, but there is no general rule for inferring running time from the barrel count: the concrete figure is what the brand publishes for each model (see [power reserve](/en/glossary/power-reserve/)).
