**The bezel** is the **metal ring** that surrounds a watch's dial, sitting between the dial and the crystal.

## Common bezel types

- **Fixed bezel** — does not rotate; purely decorative, or there to protect the crystal.
- **Unidirectional rotating bezel** — turns one way only, the signature of **dive watches** (the Rolex Submariner, for example). The logic: if the bezel gets bumped by accident, the diver reads **less** remaining time than there really is — the failure mode errs on the safe side.
- **Bidirectional rotating bezel** — turns both ways, most often seen on GMT watches.
- **Tachymeter bezel** — carries a speed scale (km/h read against the seconds taken over a measured distance), the trademark of the Omega Speedmaster.

## Materials

A bezel can be made from the same metal as the case, or from **ceramic** — a premium material with outstanding scratch resistance. Rolex calls its ceramic Cerachrom.

## Why it matters

The bezel is one of the parts that most strongly **shapes a watch's silhouette and character**. A Submariner without its glossy black rotating bezel would no longer read as a Submariner.
