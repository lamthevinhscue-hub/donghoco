**The bridge** is a **metal bar** mounted on the movement's visible side, holding the upper end of the wheel-train pivots. Remove every bridge and the whole movement comes apart — the bridges are the spine that keeps every part in its place.

## The role

Every wheel pivot needs two bearing points: one in the main plate below, one in a bridge above. The bridge is screwed down onto the main plate, clamping the pivot between them — the arbor spins freely but cannot wander.

A movement has **several bridges**, each holding a group of arbors: the escape-wheel bridge, the balance bridge, the barrel bridge... The way bridges are arranged and shaped creates the **visual identity** of each school of watchmaking — the German three-quarter plate, for instance, uses one large plate covering nearly the whole movement, very different from the many small bridges of the Swiss style.

## Where craftsmanship shows

The bridges are the largest surfaces visible when the case back comes off, which makes them the clearest stage for **finishing skill**: Geneva stripes (Côtes de Genève), anglage, striping, polished screw heads. Two movements of the same design can sit worlds apart in class purely because of the finishing level on their bridges.
