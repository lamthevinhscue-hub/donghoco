A **calibre** (sometimes spelled "caliber") is the designation of a **specific movement design** — a particular engine that a brand or movement maker produces.

## Why calibres need names

A single brand may produce many different movements, each with its own functions, accuracy and architecture. To tell them apart, each design gets a code:

- **Rolex 3235**
- **ETA 2824-2** (a very common Swiss workhorse made by the movement factory ETA and used by many brands)
- **Seiko NH35**

When someone says a watch "runs the 3235", that single number tells a knowledgeable reader a lot about its power reserve, architecture and service profile.

## What "in-house" means

When a brand **designs and manufactures its own movement**, it is called an **in-house** calibre. By contrast, many smaller brands buy ready-made movements (such as the ETA 2824) from large movement manufacturers — often called "off-the-shelf" or ébauche-based movements.

In-house production is often taken as a sign of serious watchmaking, but it is not automatically better than a proven, well-served bought-in movement.

## Worth knowing

- Movements come in two broad kinds: **mechanical (manual)** and **automatic** (self-winding).
- Each calibre has its own **beat rate** — which shapes how smoothly the seconds hand moves.
