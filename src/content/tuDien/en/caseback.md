**The caseback** is **the back of the watch case** — the side resting against the wrist. Two main types exist, each with clear strengths and trade-offs.

## The solid caseback

A sealed metal back; the movement stays hidden. The advantages: **better protection** — against knocks and dust — and usually better water resistance (fewer openings). Many deep-diving watches and tool watches use a solid back for exactly these reasons. The drawback: nothing to see.

The inside of a solid back often carries engraving: the serial number, the water-resistance in metres, sometimes decorative engraving or the crest of a military unit (on vintage military watches).

## The display caseback

A back fitted with a **transparent sapphire window**, looking straight into the movement. The advantage: half the pleasure of a mechanical watch — watching the balance swing, the finished bridges, the rotor turning (on an automatic). The drawback: the glass-to-case joint is a weak point for both water and shock, which is why deep-diving watches rarely have display backs.

Some high-end models offer both: supplied with two interchangeable backs — the solid one for diving, the display one for admiring the movement.
