**Chronometer** carries two meanings — and mixing them up causes confusion. In everyday language, a chronometer is simply "a very precise timekeeper". In modern mechanical watches, **Chronometer refers to a precision certification** — and that is the meaning worth separating out.

## COSC — the certification body

According to COSC's official pages (Contrôle Officiel Suisse des Chronomètres), the "Chronometer Certified" title is certified by an **official, neutral authority** according to the **ISO 3159** standard; each certified chronometer carries a **unique number engraved on its movement**, accompanied by a certificate issued by COSC. COSC mainly certifies **mechanical wristwatches**, alongside a few other instrument types.

On duration: COSC's page states each movement undergoes temperature and position variations **over 12 to 20 days, depending on its type**. For mechanical wristwatches specifically, the COSC FAQ states the watch is tested over **15 days**, in **five positions** and at **three temperatures (8°, 23° and 38°C)**, against **seven certification criteria** — among them the *Average Daily Rate*, which per COSC must stay **between -4 and +6 seconds per day**.

## Chronograph or chronometer?

COSC's own definition, from its page dedicated to the distinction:

> "A chronograph is a complication on a watch that makes it possible to measure an interval of time. A chronometer is a high-precision watch that has been tested and certified by the COSC."

- **Chronograph** — a timing function that measures an interval of time (see [chronograph](/en/glossary/chronograph/)).
- **Chronometer** — a watch that has been tested and certified for precision.

The words look alike and mean entirely different things — a watch can be a chronograph without being a chronometer, and the other way around. COSC also notes there is presently no dedicated standard for certifying the precision of a chronograph.

## How to read the title

- COSC currently offers **two certification levels**: "Certified Chronometer" (per COSC's page, *generally tested on the movement*) and a second level, "Excellence Chronometer Certified" (tested on the finished watch).
- A certification reflects **the result of a test, at a point in time, within that test's scope** — it does not describe the watch's behaviour in every wearing situation. The COSC FAQ states the designation is acquired for life, "even if one's performance is likely to change somewhat over time".
- "Chronometer" is the name of a certification, not a scale for comparing watches — and not every watch bearing the word follows the same process; each brand's documentation tells you what its model was certified against.
