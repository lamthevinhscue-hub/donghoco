**The clasp** is **the part that joins and opens the strap**. On a leather strap the clasp sits at the strap's end; on a metal bracelet it sits mid-way. The clasp style affects both security and wearing comfort.

## The common clasp styles

- **Tang buckle** — like a belt buckle: a pin through a punched hole in the leather. Simple, light, easy when swapping straps. The drawback: if the pin slips out of its hole the strap can open by itself — and the leather gradually wears at the punched hole.

- **Folding clasp** — the leather strap anchors to two metal halves that fold over each other: unfold to put the watch on, fold to close. Safer than a tang buckle (it will not open on its own), and because the leather is never punched it lasts longer. The drawbacks: heavier, and the wrist size must fit the fixed loop.

- **Butterfly (deployant) clasp** — a folding variant that opens on both wings, spreading the strap apart when taken off. More comfortable to wear since the wrist does not thread through the strap loop.

- **Diver's clasp** — adjusts in length by sliding, with an extra safety catch. Typical on metal-bracelet dive watches, to wear over a wetsuit.

## How to choose

A folding or butterfly clasp is the safer choice and kinder to the strap — worth considering on an expensive watch. A tang buckle is entirely adequate on an inexpensive watch with leather straps that are easy to replace.
