The **crown** is the **rotating button on the side of the case** — FHH describes it as a **knurled or fluted** button, held between thumb and forefinger, **used to wind the watch**. It is the main mechanical interface between you and the movement — winding, setting the time and the date all go through the crown.

## Two main functions

- **Winding** — turning the crown coils the [mainspring](/en/glossary/mainspring/) (on hand-wound watches; on automatics when a restart is needed). For the full method, see [winding a mechanical watch](/en/guides/winding-a-mechanical-watch/).
- **Setting the time and date** — pull the crown out one or several clicks (the number depends on the calibre), turn to set the hands, push it back when done.

## Screw-down crowns and water resistance

Water-resistant watches usually carry a **screw-down crown** — a crown that screws into the case, sealing together with the gaskets. OMEGA states the usage rule explicitly: "**If your watch is equipped with a screw-in crown, always screw it in after handling it**" — forgetting leaves a way in for water when washing hands or in the rain (see [water resistance: what you can and cannot do](/en/guides/water-resistance/) and [how water resistance works](/en/mechanisms/water-resistance/)). For a plain (non-screwing) crown, OMEGA advises pushing it flush against the case in the wearing position. Seiko adds: do not wash a watch while the crown is pulled out.

## Design details

Many crowns carry knurling for grip (as FHH describes). Some crowns are set with a cabochon stone as a decorative accent. Some crowns integrate a pusher for a chronograph — per FHH. Crown size and shape are a design choice of each model.
