**The Poinçon de Genève** (the Geneva Seal) is the **most demanding certification of finishing and origin** in Swiss watchmaking. Unlike [COSC](/en/glossary/chronometer/) certification, which measures accuracy alone, the Poinçon de Genève requires **both the place of production and the way the work is done**.

## The two core conditions

1. **Origin** — the movement must be **assembled in the canton of Geneva**. This is a geographic condition: only workshops inside the canton's borders may carry the mark. The list of brands qualifying for the Poinçon de Genève is therefore short.

2. **Finishing** — the movement must meet **twelve detailed finishing criteria**: polished [anglage](/en/glossary/anglage/) (including internal angles no machine can reach), hand-finished [bridges](/en/glossary/bridge/), chamfered screw heads, polished jewel sinks... Every component has its own specific finishing requirements.

## The mark on the watch

Once it qualifies, the movement receives the **Geneva emblem** (the eagle of Geneva) stamped on a visible [bridge](/en/glossary/bridge/). The mark is a pledge of craftsmanship — and the reason watches carrying the Poinçon de Genève are always expensive: the finishing effort multiplies, two- and threefold.

The Poinçon de Genève is not widespread — only a small share of the movements on the market qualify. It is a certificate of hand finishing, not of pure accuracy.
