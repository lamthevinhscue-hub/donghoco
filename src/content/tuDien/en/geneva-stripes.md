**Côtes de Genève** (*Geneva stripes*) is a surface decoration of **undulating lines, like waves** (per FHH). In FHH's encyclopedia article on movement decoration, Côtes de Genève is named as one example of **evenly-spaced, parallel lines, either straight or circular**, achieved with a **boxwood pad and a lathe** on the surfaces of larger parts such as **bridges** (per FHH).

## The term's scope

In the Poinçon de Genève's published requirements for baseplates, plates for additional mechanisms and bridges, **the tops of bridges** must be finished with Geneva stripes — **or any other decoration** that eliminates machining marks (per the Poinçon de Genève). The standard therefore does not tie overall quality to one single pattern: its published aim is eliminating machining marks, with several decorations meeting that aim.

Côtes de Genève names a surface treatment — it is not a certification, and not an overall quality assessment of a watch.
