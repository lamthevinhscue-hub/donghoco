**Guilloché** is a style of engraving **by hand or by machine** with intersecting **wavy or straight lines** (per FHH). The guilloché effect results when the workpiece moves **horizontally or vertically** against the tool; FHH distinguishes it from flinqué/flinking, which follows a **radial** movement (per FHH).

## The term's scope

The term describes a **surface pattern and the technique that forms it** — intersecting lines, direction of movement against the tool — not the skill involved in a given part, its value, or the overall quality of the watch carrying it (per FHH). In FHH's description of movement decoration, engraving appears alongside polishing, brushing, satin finishing, stippling, sunray decoration, snailing and chamfering (per FHH).

Guilloché names a surface treatment — it is not a certification, and not an overall quality assessment of a watch.
