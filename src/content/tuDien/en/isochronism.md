## The definition

FHH (Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie) defines it: "The property of being isochronal." — **isochronism** is the property of oscillating with the **same period**. The same page states the goal: "The science of timing lies in achieving isochronism of the regulating organ of a timekeeping instrument."

## What impairs it

Per the same FHH page: "The principal factors that can impair a balance's isochronism are the escapement, incorrect poising of the balance and spring" — the main factors are the **escapement**, incorrect **poising of the balance** and the **spring**.

## A chronomètre maker's perspective

Chronométrie Ferdinand Berthoud describes its power transmission on the maker's page: "The latter serves to deliver constant force to the escapement, a guarantee of isochronism throughout the running time of the movement, from the first turn of the crown through to complete winding…" — per the maker, steady force to the escapement is the condition that guarantees isochronism across the running time. This describes that maker's fusee-and-chain construction; it is not a general claim about all architectures.

## Reading limits

- The FHH source ties isochronism to no certification threshold (the COSC FAQ does not mention isochronism at all).
- The constant-force link above is one maker's description of one construction.

## In short

- Isochronism = oscillating with the same period; the core goal of the regulating organ (FHH).
- Escapement, incorrect poising and the spring are the named impairing factors (FHH).
- One maker (Ferdinand Berthoud) describes steady delivered force as its guarantee of isochronism.
