FHH (Fondation de la Haute Horlogerie) defines it: "The property of being isochronal" — **isochronism** is the property of oscillating with the **same period**. The same source states the goal: "The science of timing lies in achieving isochronism of the regulating organ of a timekeeping instrument".

## What impairs it

Per the same FHH page: "The principal factors that can impair a balance's isochronism are the escapement, incorrect poising of the balance and spring" — the three main factors are the **escapement**, an incorrectly **poised balance** and the **spring**.

## One chronomètre maker's view

Chronométrie Ferdinand Berthoud describes its stable-force delivery: "The latter serves to deliver constant force to the escapement, a guarantee of isochronism throughout the running time of the movement, from the first turn of the crown through to complete winding…" — per the maker's page, steady force at the escapement is the guarantee of isochronism across the running time.
