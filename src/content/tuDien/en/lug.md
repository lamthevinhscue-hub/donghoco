**The lugs** are the **protruding extensions** at the two ends of the watch case, where the strap attaches. Tilt a watch to its side and you will see the case's four corners stretch out into four horns — those are the lugs.

## Lug width — the number that decides

The specification that matters most is not the lug's shape but the **distance between the two lugs** (lug width) — measured in millimetres, 20 mm or 22 mm for example. This number decides **the strap width** you can buy: a 20 mm strap only fits a case with a 20 mm lug width. When buying a replacement strap, this is the first thing to measure.

Case diameter only tells you whether the watch is large or small; lug width tells you the **strap-to-case ratio** — and that ratio shapes the look on the wrist far more than most people expect.

## Curvature — how it hugs

Straight lugs (as on many sports watches) extend outward and then bend sharply down — this style sits flatter on a larger wrist. Curved lugs (as on many dress watches) bend gently from the case out to the tips — this style hugs a smaller wrist more comfortably but suits fewer wrist shapes.

A watch with a perfect case diameter can still wear poorly if the lugs are too long or too straight — which is why the lugs matter far more than their modest size suggests.
