The **mainspring** is a **coiled steel ribbon** housed inside the **barrel** — the cylindrical box that also carries the barrel wheel. It is the **energy source** of a mechanical watch — no battery, no electricity: all the energy that moves the hands comes from this ribbon of steel.

## The principle

Per FHH, the mainspring is **hooked to the barrel at its outer extremity and to the arbor at its inner extremity**. When you wind the watch (by hand through the [crown](/en/glossary/crown/), or automatically through the rotor), the ribbon coils tighter — energy is **stored** as elastic potential energy. As it releases, the mainspring pulls the barrel around; the **barrel wheel meshes with the first pinion of the geartrain** — the force passes through the wheel train to run the watch.

The mainspring releases **slowly**, not all at once — the escapement and balance wheel regulate it beat by beat. Much of the art of watchmaking lies in controlling the release speed of this steel ribbon.

## Why it matters

The design of the mainspring and the barrel relates directly to the **power reserve** — how long the watch runs after a full wind. The specific construction — material, length, the way it is hooked — is a design choice of each manufacturer.
