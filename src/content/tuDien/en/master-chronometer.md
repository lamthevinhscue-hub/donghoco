**Master Chronometer** is a certification established by **METAS**, the Swiss federal institute of metrology. According to METAS's official page, the certification is **carried out by an independent organization on the watch head** — the complete watch, not a bare movement.

## What the certification covers

METAS states the certification essentially covers:

- **Water resistance**;
- **Chronometric performance** (accuracy as measured by the certification's protocol);
- **Resistance to magnetic fields**;
- **Power reserve** — how long the watch runs once fully wound ([mainspring](/en/glossary/mainspring/)).

On magnetic resistance: the requirement document for this certification (**METAS N001**) specifies resistance to magnetic fields of **1.5 T (15,000 G)**. That is a **requirement within the scope of the MASTER CHRONOMETER certification — not a general level for mechanical watches**.

## Which brands can apply it?

According to METAS, the certification is open to **all brands** whose "Swiss Made" watches carry a movement certified as a **chronometer under ISO 3159**. The movement-level prerequisite of this certification therefore links to another certification — see [Chronometer](/en/glossary/chronometer/).

## COSC and Master Chronometer — two different scopes

These are **two certifications from two different organisations, with two different testing scopes**: one is generally tested on the movement (per COSC's description), the other on the complete watch (per METAS's description). Each has its own scope and criteria — this article does **not rank** one certification above the other.

For any specific model, the reliable source on what it is certified against is **the documentation of the brand that makes it**. For the magnetic-resistance dimension in particular, see [Anti-magnetism](/en/mechanisms/anti-magnetism/).
