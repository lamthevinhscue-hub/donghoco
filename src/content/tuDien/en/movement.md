The **movement** is the entire inner mechanism of a watch — everything that makes it run. It comprises the mainspring, the gear train, the escapement, the balance wheel and, on an automatic, the rotor.

Think of it as the engine of a car: you rarely see it, but it is the single most important factor in how well the watch performs.

## Three main types of movement

### 1. Quartz (battery)
- Runs on a battery, with a vibrating quartz crystal as the timekeeping element.
- Extremely accurate (seconds per month), affordable, and needs no servicing.
- But it has no mechanical soul — which is why many enthusiasts keep coming back to mechanics.

### 2. Mechanical (manual winding)
- Powered by a mainspring that you **wind by hand**, typically every day or two.
- Has the character enthusiasts prize: the ticking, the sense of a living machine.
- Needs periodic servicing to stay healthy over decades.

### 3. Automatic (self-winding)
- A mechanical movement that **winds itself** through a spinning rotor as you wear it.
- More convenient than a manual, but it still needs the same long-term care.

## Main components

- **Mainspring** — the energy source.
- **Gear train** — transmits and slows down the energy.
- **Escapement** — divides the energy into even beats.
- **Balance wheel** — governs the rate.
- **Rotor** — (automatic only) winds the mainspring from wrist motion.

## Seeing a movement

Many watches offer an **exhibition caseback** — a sapphire window onto the movement. Watching the wheels turn, the Côtes de Genève stripes and the rotor sweep is one of the quiet pleasures of owning a mechanical watch.
