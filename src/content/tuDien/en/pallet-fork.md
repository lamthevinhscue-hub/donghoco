**The pallet fork** (also called the pallet lever) is the **anchor-shaped part** sitting between the [escape wheel](/en/glossary/escape-wheel/) and the [balance](/en/glossary/hairspring/). It does two jobs at once: **locking and releasing the escape wheel step by step**, and **passing impulse to the balance**.

## How it works

Picture the pallet fork as a two-way gate rocking back and forth. Two jewels (the pallet jewels) sit at its two ends — one in, one out — touching the escape wheel's teeth.

When the [balance](/en/glossary/hairspring/) swings to the left, it nudges the fork to the left. The fork releases one escape tooth; the wheel turns a little before the fork's other jewel catches it. At the instant the wheel "escapes", it pushes the fork — and that push travels back up into the balance, **topping up its energy** so the oscillation does not die out.

The cycle repeats continuously: balance to the left → the fork releases → the escape wheel jumps one tooth → the fork locks again → balance to the right → repeat. Each such beat is one tick.

## Recognizing it

Watching a running movement, the pallet fork is the part that **rocks back and forth constantly, fastest of all** — it flicks from side to side at twice the balance's rate. It is one of the most immediately visible parts in a working movement.
