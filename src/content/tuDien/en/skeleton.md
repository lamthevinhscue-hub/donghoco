**Skeleton work** is **cutting away most of the excess material** of the bridges and main plate so the movement can be seen straight through — you look at the gear train, the barrel and the balance across the openwork. Carried further, the French term *squelette* names the fully openworked watch, where even the dial is carved away until only a few numerals float.

## The hard part

Skeleton work sounds like "cutting holes", but the real difficulty is **removing material while keeping rigidity**. A bridge must stay stiff enough to hold the wheel pivots in position — carve away too much and the bridge flexes, the pivots drift, the watch loses accuracy. That is why a genuine skeleton is redesigned as a structure from the start, not carved out of a finished movement afterwards.

What is more, every cut edge must be **finished** — all of them are now exposed, none left hidden. Anglage, Geneva stripes and polishing have to be carried out on both faces of every bridge. That is why a true skeleton always costs: the finishing effort doubles.

## What it is not

- **Display caseback** — the movement is admired through the back, but it is an ordinary, uncut movement.
- **Open-heart** — a small aperture over the balance; the rest of the dial stays solid.
- **Skeleton** — the whole movement is openworked, visible straight through.

A skeleton is a declaration: *"nothing to hide"* — every part is on display, and every part has to be beautiful.
