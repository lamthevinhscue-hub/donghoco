A **tachymeter scale** is a series of numbers placed on the bezel, on the rehaut (the inner edge of the dial) or on the dial itself, depending on the model, used together with a [chronograph](/en/glossary/chronograph/) to **read average speed** over a fixed distance — per OMEGA's definition, the reading is km/h based on a 1000-metre distance. FHH lists the tachymeter among the measurement scales attached to chronographs (alongside the telemeter and the pulsometer). The exact range of numbers varies by model — the scale illustrated in the [tachymeter guide](/en/guides/using-a-tachymeter/) runs from 60 to 400 — and the scale is **not evenly spaced**: that is not a defect.

## How it is used

You start the chronograph as an object (a car, a runner) passes the start point, and stop it once the object has covered a known distance (usually 1 km or 1 mile). The seconds hand points to a number on the tachymeter scale — that is the **average speed** (units per hour) over the distance just measured.

Example: if a car takes 30 seconds to cover 1 km, the hand points to 120 on the scale — an average of 120 km/h.

## Why is the scale not evenly spaced?

On a standard scale calibrated for one unit of distance, the arithmetic behind it is: speed (km/h) = 3600 ÷ time in seconds to cover 1 km — following from OMEGA's "km/h based on a 1000-metre distance" definition. The seconds hand sweeps uniformly with time, so the spacing of the marks on the dial follows time: 5 seconds and 6 seconds are only 6° apart — the two marks sit close together, even though the speed values differ from 720 down to 600 km/h; 50 seconds and 55 seconds are 30° apart — the two marks sit far apart, even though the values differ from 72 to about 65 km/h. That is why the short-time/high-speed region has its marks placed close together, and the long-time/low-speed region has its marks placed farther apart. This is a signature of a real tachymeter scale.

## Limits of reading

The scale reads directly only while the measured time stays within the range of numbers printed on that model's scale — outside that range, the hand no longer corresponds to a number. By the same principle, a tachymeter scale can also be used for other quantities expressed in units per hour (production pace, for example).
